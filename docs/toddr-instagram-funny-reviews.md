# Instagram: funny reviews account

Idea from Todd: an Instagram account that finds funny customer reviews and aggregates them into
posts, as a top-of-funnel for Toddr.ai. This format has a track record (accounts that post absurd
Amazon, Yelp and Google reviews get large followings), so the idea is sound. The notes below are
about doing it in a way that does not backfire on a company whose whole pitch is "reputation".

## Guardrails (decide these before the first post)

1. **Punch up, not down.** Funny reviews of big chains, airlines, theme parks, national brands:
   fine. Funny reviews that mock a small local business by name: do not. Those are the exact
   people you want as customers, and screenshots travel. Blur or crop the business name when it
   is a small business, or skip the review.
2. **Blur reviewer names and photos.** Reviews are public, but reposting a named person for
   laughs invites complaints and occasional takedowns. The review text and star rating are the
   joke; the name is not.
3. **Copyright.** The reviewer wrote the text and technically holds the copyright. Reposting
   short reviews with commentary is generally treated as fair use in practice by the many accounts
   that do this, but I am not certain it would hold in every case and I am not a lawyer. Keep
   reviews short, add your own caption or commentary, and take down anything on request.
4. **Never fabricate a review for a post.** The account belongs to a reviews company; one faked
   "funny review" that gets caught ends the account's credibility and the company's.
5. **No reviews involving kids, medical details, or anything identifying a private person.**
6. **Source only from public pages you view normally.** No scraping tools against Google or Yelp;
   both prohibit it in their terms.

## Format

- Account name: something generic and funny, not "toddr.ai", so it reads as entertainment first.
  Bio links to toddr.ai with a line like "we help good businesses get the reviews they deserve".
- Post: clean screenshot (or re-typed in a branded card) of the review, star rating visible, one
  caption line. Carousel of 3 to 5 reviews on a theme ("reviews that are technically about the
  parking lot") performs better than singles, as I understand the format; test both.
- Every 5th post is a soft Toddr.ai post: a real (permissioned) good review turned into one of
  the product's generated images, captioned "this one was made by Toddr.ai in 4 seconds".
  The product's own output is the ad.
- Reels: read-aloud of the week's best three reviews. Cheap to make, and the product's video
  feature can eventually make these.

## Cadence and effort

- 1 post per day for the first 30 days, then review. Batch-create a week of posts in one sitting.
- Keep a spreadsheet of candidate reviews with source link, date saved, and whether the business
  is a chain (ok) or small (blur/skip).
- Reply to every comment for the first month; that is what the algorithm rewards early.

## What to measure

- Followers, saves and shares per post (saves and shares matter more than likes).
- Link clicks from the bio to toddr.ai, and signups that came from Instagram (use a UTM-tagged
  link so they are counted separately).
- Decide at day 30 whether to continue based on clicks, not followers.

## Open question

Who runs it day to day? It needs a consistent voice. If that is you, budget about 3 hours a
week. If you want to automate the hunting part, that is something that could be built, but
only against sources whose terms allow it.
